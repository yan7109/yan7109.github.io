The Toughest Sell A Founder's Guide to Startup Exits

Acknowledgements

My startup journey is a rather non-typical one. It took over ten years to achieve what could have been accomplished in a much shorter amount of time if I had focused only on the final financial outcome. However, everything that happened in between, the highest highs and the lowest lows, the rush of seeing strangers on the bus using our apps, noticing CVPR attendees taking photos using the shot suggestion feature we developed for Samsung, and catching up with former team members who now lead armies of researchers and engineers at frontier labs, always feel like they would have taken a lifetime to accomplish. For that I am always grateful, and it reminds me again of why it was all worth it.

This book only captures a glimpse of that entire journey. If the ten years of founding, building, recruiting, fundraising, scaling, pivoting, and eventually selling is a marathon, the M&A process is merely the very last mile, one that took every fiber of my being and the collective effort of my family, cofounder, board members, investors, and employees to ultimately complete. It was the most uncertain, emotional, and turbulent chapter of my professional life.

To my wife Charis, you were the constant during this entire time. Your steadfast love and support held our family steady when I couldn't. Thank you for believing in me when I didn't believe in myself. To our kids Wesley and Chelsea, I hope one day when you grow up, you'll learn to appreciate that we do things not because they are expedient or give instant gratification, but because they are hard, because we may fail spectacularly, because others will think we are crazy, because often there will not be a prize in the end, and finally because it is the right thing to do.

To my parents, thank you for instilling your values of hard work, persistence, curiosity, and humility in me. During the difficult times, I am always reminded that the two of you left your tenured positions in your late thirties at university and started from scratch in Canada, worked at the very bottom of society packing groceries and working off of assembly lines so that I could have a much better education and far more favorable path to fulfill my full potential.

To my parents-in-law, thank you for the years of supporting our family and our kids, keeping everything spotless in our household and always preparing gourmet meals for us all through these years.

To my cofounders Borui Wang, Enhao Gong, and Xiao Liang, we built Polarr together from nothing into a consumer brand recognized by tens of millions of photographers, and ended up powering the camera experiences on over a hundred million devices. There is no version of this story without you. And to Borui especially, thank you for your thoughtfulness, compassion, generosity, critical feedback when I needed it the most, and everything else I could ask for from a cofounder, that working together does not feel like work but more like play, even during the most difficult times.

Finally, as I wrap this up, I am reminded of the parable of the Talents told by Christ, where a master entrusts three of his servants with varying amounts of money based on their abilities before leaving on a journey. Two of the servants invested and doubled the money, and upon the master's return they received praise and even more to manage. The last one hid the money out of fear, and was admonished for having nothing to show for it. In writing this book and reflecting on the people named here, I am struck by how much has been entrusted to me: the faith of investors, the years of my cofounders, the patience of my board, the sacrifice of my family, the dedication of every employee who chose to spend a part of their career at Polarr. These were not mine to keep. They were mine to steward, to invest, to multiply. I hope that in the end, I did not bury what was given to me, but put it to work, and that the fruits of this journey will continue to grow and touch others a hundred and a thousand fold.
